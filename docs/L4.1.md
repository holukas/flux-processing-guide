---
title: "L4.1 · Gap-filling"
---

Fills gaps in the quality-controlled fluxes, using the meteo drivers and, if relevant, management data.

## Methods

- **MDS** (marginal distribution sampling): the default method for NEE, water and energy fluxes, with `SW_IN`, `TA` and `VPD` as drivers (Reichstein et al., 2005).
- **N2O and CH4:** there is no agreed method. Random forest approaches work well (Maier et al., 2022).
- **Long-term random forest** ([diive](scripts/diive.md)): one model per year, trained on that year and its two neighbouring years. The first and last year use the two closest years.
- **XGBoost** ([diive](scripts/diive.md)): can also be used for the gap-filling. *To be written.*
- **Exceptions:** where the random forest does poorly, e.g. right after a strong disturbance, MDS can fill that period instead.

## Random forest features

- **Drivers per flux:** e.g. air temperature, radiation and VPD for NEE, LE and H; soil temperature, soil water content and precipitation for N2O and CH4.
- **Lagged variants:** e.g. the mean over the preceding 3 hours, or that mean shifted back by 3, 6, 9 and 12 hours.
- **Time since an event:** the number of records since the last precipitation or management event. See [Management data](Management_Data.md).
- **Timestamp information:** e.g. year, season, month, day of year, hour.
- **Feature reduction:** a random variable is added as a feature. Features less important than it are removed.
- **Importance:** permutation importance, not the default Gini importance, which can mislead for features with many unique values.
- **Reproducible:** a fixed random seed.
- **Few measured fluxes:** for a year with few measured fluxes, a model trained on all years can fill that year.
