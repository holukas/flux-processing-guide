[fluxrun](scripts/fluxrun.md) runs EddyPro with relaxed settings. The preliminary fluxes are used for checks and help to find the best settings for the [L1 final flux run](L1.md).

- **Boxes:** 4 and 5 in the [processing chain](Processing_Chain.md).
- **Input:** raw data CSV files from [bico](Raw_Data_Conversion.md) or [rECord](Raw_Data_Logging.md), compressed (`.gz`) or not.
- **Settings:** an EddyPro settings file (`.eddypro`) with a `.metadata` file of the same name next to it.
- **Output:** preliminary fluxes.
- **Runs on:** your own computer. It can also run automatically, e.g. once a day.
- **Next step:** [L1 final flux run](L1.md).

## No strict rules

- L0 has no fixed settings. They depend on what you want to check.
- Some settings may not be final yet, e.g. the vertical separation of the gas analyzer.
- A faster spectral correction saves time, e.g. Moncrieff et al. (1997) instead of Fratini et al. (2012).

## What the L0 fluxes are used for

- **Check the site:** are the data complete, do the fluxes look plausible?
- **Time lags:** find the time lag window for the L1 run.
- **Wind direction:** compare the wind direction across years.

## Time lags (OPENLAG)

- **When:** needed when it is not known where the lag lies, e.g. for a new instrument.
- **Wide window:** L0 searches the time lag between the vertical wind and the gas in a wide window, e.g. -1 s to +10 s. This run is called OPENLAG.
- **No nominal lag:** L0 uses covariance maximization only, without a nominal (default) time lag.
- **Start at -1 s:** with a start value of 0 s, EddyPro started the lag search later, e.g. at +1 s.
- **Narrow down:** if the lags cluster in a smaller range, a second OPENLAG run with a narrower window gives a clearer peak.
- **Histogram:** the peak of the histogram of the found lags is the most frequent lag. The L1 run uses it:
  - **IRGA (CO2, H2O):** the peak is the nominal lag, with a narrow window around it.
  - **QCL and LGR (N2O, CH4):** the peak is a constant lag, without a window.
- **Different gases:** each gas can have its own lag. In closed-path analyzers, the lag of H2O is generally longer than that of CO2.
- **Two peaks:** a histogram with two peaks means that the lag changed. Split the data into periods and give each period its own lag.

## Wind direction

- Compare the histogram of the wind direction of each year with a reference period.
- The offset is the number of degrees to add to the wind direction of a year to get the histogram closest to the reference.
- Small offsets across all years mean that the orientation of the sonic anemometer stayed the same.
